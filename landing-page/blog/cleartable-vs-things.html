Cleartable vs Things 3: Calm Task Apps Compared

October 6, 2026 · 7 min read · by the Cleartable team

Things 3 and Cleartable share a philosophy: task management should feel calm, not overwhelming. Both apps bet on simplicity over feature overload. But they get there in very different ways.

Things 3 is a beautifully crafted native Apple app that trusts you to organize your own tasks with elegant drag-and-drop. Cleartable is a web app that uses AI to do the organizing for you, and adds habit tracking and calendar planning into the same surface.

If you're drawn to calm, focused task management, both of these deserve a look. Here's how they compare.

Where Things 3 wins

Things 3 is a masterclass in native app design. Here's where it's genuinely better:

  • Best-in-class Apple experience. Things 3 is one of the most beautiful apps on any platform. Animations, gestures, haptics — everything feels considered. If you live in the Apple ecosystem and care about craft, nothing else comes close.
  • One-time purchase. Pay once, use forever. No subscription, no tier anxiety. $50 for Mac and $10 for iPhone is a fair deal for years of use. Cleartable is $4/month, which adds up over time.
  • True offline-first. Things stores everything locally and syncs via its own cloud. It works perfectly without internet. Cleartable works offline once installed on your device (you add it to your home screen and it runs full-screen like a native app), but it's a web app at heart.
  • Quick Entry. The global keyboard shortcut that captures a task from anywhere on your Mac is fast and beloved. It's one of those small features that changes how you work.
  • Simplicity by design. Things deliberately leaves features out. No labels, no filters, no integrations. For people who find those things distracting, that restraint is the feature.

Where Cleartable wins

Cleartable takes the calm philosophy and adds intelligence:

  • AI does the filing. Things trusts you to drag tasks into the right project and set the right date. Cleartable does that work for you — you type a thought, AI sorts it, you confirm with a tap. Less manual overhead means the system stays organized even on busy weeks.
  • Works everywhere. Things is Apple-only. If you use a Windows PC at work, an Android phone, or a Chromebook, Things isn't an option. Cleartable runs in any browser and can be installed on any phone or computer — add it to your home screen and it opens full-screen, just like a native app.
  • Habit tracking, built in. Things doesn't track habits. If you want tasks and habits in one view, you need a separate app alongside Things. Cleartable has streaks, skip days, interval scheduling, and quit-habit tracking built in.
  • Calendar-aware planning. Things shows your Apple Calendar events alongside tasks (read-only). Cleartable syncs with Google Calendar and builds your daily plan around your meetings — fitting tasks into the free slots so you don't overbook yourself.
  • Weekly review. The weekly review is where task systems either stay alive or quietly die. Cleartable has an AI-assisted review built in. Things leaves it entirely to you.
  • Reference lists. Books to read, restaurants to try, gift ideas — Cleartable handles these with custom lists that include ratings and AI-powered link extraction. Things doesn't have a concept of reference lists.

Pick Things 3 if…

You live in the Apple ecosystem and won't leave. You love beautifully crafted native apps. You prefer to organize tasks yourself with drag-and-drop, and you don't need AI, habits, or calendar planning. You want to pay once and never think about it again.

Pick Cleartable if…

You want AI to handle the organizing so you don't have to. You use a mix of Apple and non-Apple devices. You track habits and want them alongside your tasks. You like the idea of a daily plan that's built around your actual calendar. You want a calm app that also happens to be smart.

A note on philosophy

Things 3 and Cleartable are both opinionated about calm. Things achieves it through restraint — fewer features, less noise. Cleartable achieves it through automation — AI handles the busywork so you see a clean surface. Both approaches work. The question is whether you'd rather organize yourself or have the app do it for you.

Cleartable is a young app, actively developed and shipping new features every week. If something is missing today, it may land soon. You can always tell us what matters to you — at this stage, every message shapes what gets built.

Frequently asked questions

Is Cleartable a good alternative to Things 3?

Cleartable is a strong alternative if you want AI-powered organization, built-in habit tracking, and calendar-aware daily planning — and you use Android or Windows alongside Apple devices. If you value the best-in-class native Apple design and offline-first simplicity, Things 3 is hard to beat.

Does Things 3 work on Windows or Android?

No. Things 3 is exclusively available on Mac, iPhone, iPad, and Apple Watch. There is no web app, Windows version, or Android app. If you use any non-Apple device, you'll need an alternative like Cleartable, which runs in any browser and can be installed on your phone's home screen to work like a native app.

Does Things 3 have AI features?

Things 3 does not include AI features. It relies on manual organization with a clean drag-and-drop interface. Cleartable uses AI to automatically sort, categorize, and prioritize tasks from plain-language input, and offers AI-powered daily planning.
