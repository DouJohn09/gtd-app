Cleartable vs TickTick: Tasks, Habits & Calendar in One App

October 6, 2026 · 7 min read · by the Cleartable team

TickTick and Cleartable are the two apps in this space that try to be your single productivity home — tasks, habits, and calendar together instead of three separate tools. That shared ambition makes this the closest comparison in the series.

The difference is philosophy. TickTick gives you every feature and lets you configure your way to productivity. Cleartable uses AI to handle the configuration for you, so the app stays simple even as your life gets complex.

Where TickTick wins

TickTick has been around since 2013 and it shows — in the best way:

  • Feature depth. Pomodoro timer, Eisenhower matrix, kanban boards, duration estimates, subtasks with progress, custom smart lists with complex filters. If you want a feature, TickTick probably has it. Cleartable is deliberately leaner.
  • Native apps everywhere. TickTick has polished native apps for iOS, Android, Mac, Windows, Apple Watch, and browser extensions. Cleartable is a web app you can install on your phone's home screen (it opens full-screen and works offline, like a native app) — functional everywhere, but not quite native polish.
  • Calendar integrations. TickTick connects to Google, Outlook, and Apple Calendar. Cleartable currently supports Google Calendar only.
  • Habit statistics. TickTick's habit tracker has detailed completion rates, charts, and historical data. Cleartable's habit tracking covers the essentials (streaks, skip days, intervals, quit habits) but doesn't match TickTick's analytics depth.
  • Collaboration. Shared lists and task assignment work well for couples, families, or small teams. Cleartable is solo-only today.
  • Pomodoro & focus tools. Built-in timer with white noise, focus stats, and integration with tasks. Cleartable doesn't have focus/timer features.

Where Cleartable wins

Cleartable trades feature count for intelligence:

  • AI does the organizing. TickTick's natural language parsing handles dates ("tomorrow at 3pm"). Cleartable goes much further — it reads your plain-language thought and figures out the project, priority, context, due date, and list placement. You capture; it sorts. The system stays organized even when you're too busy to organize.
  • AI daily planning. Every morning, Cleartable looks at your calendar, your priorities, and your overdue tasks, then builds a realistic plan that fits into your free time. TickTick shows you a list of what's due; Cleartable tells you what to do when.
  • Calm over complexity. TickTick has a lot of features, which means a lot of settings, views, and decisions. Cleartable stays simple because AI absorbs the complexity. You don't need to learn filters, smart lists, or priority systems — the app figures it out.
  • Weekly review. The habit that keeps task systems alive. Cleartable has an AI-assisted review built in. TickTick has no review workflow.
  • Reference lists. Books, restaurants, gift ideas, travel spots — Cleartable handles non-task lists with ratings and AI-powered link extraction. TickTick doesn't distinguish between task lists and reference lists.
  • Price. Cleartable Pro is $4/month ($36/year). TickTick Premium is $35.99/year. Close, but Cleartable includes AI features in that price; TickTick doesn't have comparable AI.

Pick TickTick if…

You want a feature-rich all-in-one tool and you're happy organizing things yourself. You use Pomodoro timers. You need native apps on every platform. You share lists with a partner or team. You like having every option available and configuring the app to your exact workflow.

Pick Cleartable if…

You want the same tasks-habits-calendar combo but don't want to spend time organizing it all. You'd rather type a messy thought and have AI sort it than fill in fields and pick lists. You want a daily plan built around your calendar, not just a list of what's due. You prefer calm simplicity over maximum features.

Same ambition, different approach

Both apps want to be your single productivity home. The question is how much organizing you want to do yourself. Cleartable is free to try — capture a few tasks, let AI sort them, and see if less manual work means more clarity.

The overlap — and the gap

TickTick and Cleartable are the closest competitors in this category because they share the same vision: one app for tasks, habits, and calendar. The gap is in how they get there. TickTick adds features. Cleartable adds intelligence. Both are valid paths — pick the one that matches how your brain works.

Cleartable is a young app, actively developed and shipping new features every week. If something is missing today, it may land soon. You can always tell us what matters to you — at this stage, every message shapes what gets built.

Frequently asked questions

Is Cleartable a good alternative to TickTick?

Cleartable is a strong alternative if you want AI to handle the organizing for you instead of managing everything manually. Both apps combine tasks, habits, and calendar, but Cleartable's AI capture and daily planning mean less time sorting and more time doing. TickTick is better if you want maximum features, Pomodoro timers, and a mature native app ecosystem.

Does TickTick have AI features?

TickTick has basic natural language parsing for dates and times, similar to Todoist. It does not have AI-powered task classification, automatic project assignment, or AI daily planning. Cleartable uses AI to sort tasks into the right project, set priorities, and build a realistic daily plan around your calendar.

Which app is better for habit tracking — Cleartable or TickTick?

TickTick has more mature habit tracking with detailed statistics and a dedicated habits tab. Cleartable's habit tracking includes streaks, skip days, interval scheduling, and quit-habit tracking, which covers most needs. TickTick wins on habit analytics depth; Cleartable wins on AI integration across the whole app.
