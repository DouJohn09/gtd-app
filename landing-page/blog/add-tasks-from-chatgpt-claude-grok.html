Add Tasks to Your To-Do List from ChatGPT, Claude or Grok

October 26, 2026 · 5 min read · by the Cleartable team

A lot of planning already happens in an AI chat. You talk a messy week through with ChatGPT, ask Claude to break a project into steps, or brainstorm a weekend with Grok — and at the end there's a handful of real to-dos sitting in the conversation. Then comes the boring part: copying them into your task app one by one. Or, more honestly, not copying them, and losing half of them by Thursday.

Cleartable now closes that gap. Connect your assistant once, and from then on you can say "put this in Cleartable" in any chat. The tasks land in your Cleartable inbox, already sorted, and one tap plans them into your calendar.

What it looks like

Say you're in ChatGPT and type:

"Put these in Cleartable: book the car service, buy a gift for Mia's party on Saturday, and plan the hike with Tom."

The assistant calls Cleartable, and a few seconds later it replies that three tasks were added. In Cleartable you find three separate tasks — not one long note — each sorted the same way as anything you capture yourself: the right list and context, "by Saturday" read as a due date, a sensible time estimate. Press Plan my week and they're placed into free time around what's already in your calendar — the gift before Saturday, the hike planning on Sunday after your run.

It works the other way too. Ask "what's on my plate today in Cleartable?" and the assistant reads your day — tasks, calendar events and habits — so you can talk it through, reschedule something, or tick off what you just finished.

What the assistant can and can't do

  • Add tasks — one at a time, or a whole messy paragraph that Cleartable's AI splits into separate, sorted tasks.
  • Read your tasks, projects and today's plan.
  • Update tasks (dates, deadlines, lists, notes) and complete them.
  • Never delete anything, and never touch your account or billing.

You approve the connection on a Cleartable page that says exactly this before anything is shared, and Settings → AI apps lists every connected app with a Disconnect button.

How to connect it

Every assistant asks for the same address: https://cleartable.app/mcp. You'll also find it, with a copy button, in Cleartable under Settings → AI apps. After you add it, the assistant sends you to a Cleartable sign-in and an Allow page — that's the whole setup. Menus move around, so if a label differs slightly, look for "connectors".

Claude

  1. Open Cleartable in Claude's connector directory and press Connect. (On a Team or Enterprise plan, an owner may need to enable it first under Organization settings → Connectors.)
  2. Sign in to Cleartable and press Allow.
  3. No luck finding it? Add it by hand instead: Customize → Connectors, + Add, Add custom connector, name it Cleartable and paste the address.

ChatGPT

  1. On chatgpt.com (Plus plan or higher), open Settings → Apps → Advanced settings and turn on Developer mode.
  2. Press Create app: name it Cleartable, paste the address, choose OAuth, and confirm.
  3. Sign in to Cleartable and press Allow. In a chat, switch Cleartable on from the + menu. ChatGPT asks you to confirm each change it makes — that's expected.

Grok

  1. Go to grok.com/connectors and choose New connector → Custom.
  2. Paste the address and connect.
  3. Sign in to Cleartable and press Allow.

Why a connection, not a copy-paste

The connection uses MCP (Model Context Protocol), the open standard AI assistants use to talk to other apps. For you that means two things. First, nothing is pasted around: the assistant asks Cleartable directly, using a sign-in you approved, and that access can be revoked in one click. Second, Cleartable stays the place where your tasks are organized. The assistant is a great place to think; Cleartable is where those thoughts become a sorted list, a planned day and a calendar you can trust.

A few ways people use it

  • After a planning chat: "Add the action items from this conversation to Cleartable."
  • From a long email or document: paste it in and ask for the to-dos to go to Cleartable.
  • Morning check-in: "What's on today in Cleartable, and what should I do first?"
  • End of day: "Mark the dentist call done and move the deck to tomorrow."

Frequently asked questions

Which AI assistants can I connect to Cleartable?

ChatGPT, Claude and Grok. Cleartable runs a standard MCP server at https://cleartable.app/mcp, so any assistant that supports custom MCP connectors with sign-in can use it. In Cleartable, Settings → AI apps has the address and the steps for each one.

Can the assistant delete or mess up my tasks?

No deleting — the connection can add, read, update and complete tasks, but there is no delete at all. You approve the connection on a Cleartable page that lists exactly what it can do, and you can disconnect any app in Settings → AI apps at any time.

Do I need a paid plan?

Not on Cleartable: connecting an assistant works on the free plan, and tasks the assistant sends through Cleartable's sorting count toward your normal daily AI allowance. On the assistant's side it depends on the app. At the time of writing, Claude allows one custom connector on its free plan, Grok offers connectors to all users, and ChatGPT needs a paid plan (Plus or higher) for the developer mode that custom connectors use.

Is my data shared with ChatGPT, Claude or Grok?

Only what you ask for in that chat. The assistant reads tasks when you ask it to (for example "what's on today?"), and that reply becomes part of your conversation with it, under that assistant's own privacy terms. Cleartable never sends your tasks anywhere on its own, and disconnecting stops all access immediately.
